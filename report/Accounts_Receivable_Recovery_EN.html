Accounts Receivable Recovery: Open-Balance Gap & Collections Priority

Analysis of a 50k-row real SAP receivables extract: find the "billed vs received" reconciliation gap ($334M open), score every invoice by collections priority with ML, and put collections effort where it is most likely to be recovered.

Data: real SAP accounts receivable (2018–2020) · 50,000 lines / 48,833 invoices · total AR $1.62B
1 · Objective 2 · Cleaning 3 · Metrics & Charts 4 · Modeling 5 · Recommendations
Part 1 · Business Objective

1. Why this analysis

Finance/AR teams face 48,833 invoices / $1.62B AR, of which 10,000 (20%) are still open at the snapshot date, worth about $334M. The team cannot chase every invoice, and doesn't know whom to chase first — collections get diluted, DSO stretches, bad debt builds up.

This project answers three questions:

  • How big is the gap, where is it — the open ("billed vs received") balance, its distribution and concentration.
  • Who to chase first — score every open invoice by collections priority (open probability × amount).
  • Where is the root cause — which payment terms, business units, customers are worst for overdue, as negotiation leverage.

Reconciliation boundary. This project completes the receivables data-layer reconciliation: quantify the `clear_date vs isOpen` gap ($334M, of which $322.9M / 94.8% is already past-due-open = true overdue AR), and give a recovery order. Line-level voucher matching (invoice ↔ PO ↔ payment) needs bank statements, which this dataset lacks — listed as next step, not claimed done.

One-line goal: use a collections-priority queue + overdue root cause to put collections effort on "large amount × likely open" invoices, shortening DSO and lifting recovery rate.

Part 4 · Modeling

4. Business-driven prediction (principle · why · how)

4.1 Goal: predict "still open after due" (isOpen binary)

Why (business-driven). A rule can only say "overdue or not"; it cannot rank 10,000 open invoices by recovery risk. ML gives each invoice an "open probability", then × amount → collections priority — chase "large and likely-open" first, instead of blindly sorting by amount (which misses many small-but-recoverable invoices).

Principle. Learn the mapping features → open probability: baseline logistic regression (interpretable coefficients), then random forest / gradient boosting (trees capture interactions & nonlinearity, e.g. "high amount × high overdue × specific term").

How & leakage control. Features = log amount, posting→due days, payment term, business unit, currency, posting month, customer frequency (frequency stats from training only). Stratified random 80/20 split. Key fix: isOpen is a snapshot label strongly tied to posting time — a temporal split would put mostly recent, not-yet-due invoices in validation and AUC would fall below random (measured 0.33); so a stratified split is used, targeting "whether the invoice is ultimately open".

Feature importance (GBM): posting_month dominates (0.92) — "open" is mainly decided by posting time (recent invoices still in term). This is a true property of snapshot data, not a model flaw.

Why this matters (honestly). AUC 0.912 is high, but ~92% of the signal is "posting month" — because isOpen is a 2020-05 snapshot: only recent postings can still be open; older ones are settled. So the model reproduces "is this a recent posting that is open", not long-horizon bad-debt prediction. Its actionable value is limited to: within the past-due-open $322.9M, rank with "amount × probability" — turning snapshot info into a priority queue, not causal forecasting.

4.3 Output: collections priority queue

Collection set = validation past-due-open (2,000, $68.1M), ranked by priority = amount × open probability descending: Top 50 ≈ $8.0M, all currently open. This is a hand-off "chase-first list" for the collections team.

Queue's incremental value. posting_month only decides "which month goes first"; within the same posting month, ranking differences come from log amount, days-to-due, payment terms and other features — that ~8% of remaining signal is what makes the "amount × probability" queue better than plain "amount descending".
Method note · deep learning? The dataset is 50k rows, plain tabular, no sequence: GBM/RF already reach AUC 0.91 and are interpretable (feature importance, coefficients) — fitting the "collections story". DL (MLP etc.) adds little at this scale and hurts explainability, so it is not the main model; per my rule, DL needs enough data and is only a footnote here.
Part 5 · Recommendations

5. One actionable plan

High impact

1 · Run the collections queue in priority order

Use past-due open ($322.9M / 9,484) as the pool, order by model "amount × open probability" (validation Top50 ≈ $8.0M); chase large & already-overdue first; U001 business unit first.

High impact

2 · Renegotiate terms: NAX2 / CA10 / NAVF

NAX2 is almost always late (99.8%, avg 42.6d), CA10 is large (3,075, 75% late) — amend contracts or add late penalties to shorten aging at the source.

Cash flow

3 · Due reminders + escalation to cut the 41.9% late rate

41.9% ($571.7M) of settled invoices arrive past due. Auto-remind before due, auto-escalate after — target lowering the late share.

Next step

4 · Line-level reconciliation (invoice↔PO↔payment)

This dataset supports data-layer gap quantification ($334M, of which $322.9M past-due open) + collections ranking; voucher-level matching needs bank statements, listed as next step, not claimed done.
